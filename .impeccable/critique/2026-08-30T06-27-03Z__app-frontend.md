## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment (Assessment A):** Grounded, with generic patches. The core review screen is authored for this exact product — the per-item "Source text" disclosure directly operationalizes PRODUCT.md's verbatim-quote guard at the point of decision, section names are lifted verbatim from the real MDX template, and the command bar speaks the reviewer's task language. Where it slips into generic-admin-panel territory: the upload-history and staff-profile tables are unstyled, unsearchable, unsortable lists with no CV-specific thinking applied, and 16 of 21 review sections render as undifferentiated "not found" boilerplate.

**Deterministic scan (Assessment B):** 19 findings via `detect.mjs`, all advisory/warning (no errors) — but the scan ran in degraded mode (HTML parser modules unavailable, falling back to regex matching — computed contrast NOT evaluated), so this is a confirmed undercount, not a clean bill of health. Findings: 16 `design-system-font-size` advisories (real CSS sizes like `0.82rem`, `0.95rem`, `1.02rem` sitting between DESIGN.md's declared 3-step type scale), 2 `design-system-radius` advisories (2px and 10px outside the declared 6/8/20px scale), 1 `overused-font` warning on Arial, 1 `side-tab` warning.

**Where they connect:** the detector's contrast checks never ran — and Assessment A, working independently, found zero `aria-*` attributes, zero wired `<label for>`s, and bare-glyph checkmark/warning/cross icons with no screen-reader announcement anywhere in the frontend. These aren't two separate findings; they're the same accessibility gap seen from two angles, and the real size of it is larger than either assessment alone shows, because the one tool that could have quantified contrast automatically didn't run.

**A likely false positive:** the `overused-font: Arial` warning targets fonts that "converge" across AI-generated UIs (Inter, Roboto, Geist); Arial is a plain system font, and DESIGN.md's own "One Typeface Rule" documents this as a deliberate, single-typeface choice — not drift.

**Visual overlays:** not available. The interactive live-editing overlay (`live-browser.js`) requires a companion server (token/port/session module) that wasn't running, and Assessment B correctly declined to fake an injection rather than report a misleading result. Screenshot capture also failed in this environment — both assessments substituted DOM/accessibility-tree text extraction against the live, real-data instance instead, which is factual but not pixel-level evidence.

## Overall Impression

The system genuinely knows what it's for — the split-pane review layout and per-item source disclosure aren't decoration, they're the product's core trust mechanism made visible. What's missing isn't design sense; it's the unglamorous second pass: nothing confirms a permanent delete, nothing tells a first-timer what a confidence pill means, and the screen has no answer for what happens once a CV is complex (live data showed one document with 32 stacked Biography cards and 16 empty sections in a single unbroken scroll). The biggest single opportunity is making the review screen breathe — collapse the noise, so the dozen items that actually need judgment aren't buried in the ones that don't.

## What's Working

1. **Source-text disclosure on every item** — operationalizes the "never show a fact the CV didn't say" principle at the exact point of decision, not as a separate audit trail nobody checks.
2. **Confidence vs. coverage kept as two distinct, correctly-explained concepts** — sophisticated domain modeling ("how sure are we" vs. "how much of the CV reached a section at all") communicated in plain reviewer-facing language.
3. **A real keyboard-driven review loop** (J/K/A/E/R + natural-language command bar) for staff who do this daily — not a bolted-on shortcut list.

## Priority Issues

- **[P0] Destructive Delete has no confirmation.** Clicking Delete on a classified item removes it immediately — no confirm dialog, no undo — while the functionally lighter "Approve all remaining" does get a confirmation dialog. Why it matters: a misclick permanently destroys classified content on a document headed to HR. Fix: add the same confirm pattern already used for bulk-approve, or an inline undo toast. Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

- **[P0] Zero accessibility semantics in the frontend.** No aria-* attributes, no wired label/for anywhere; review.html's textareas, section-move dropdowns, and add-item fields are unlabeled to assistive tech, and the quality report's status glyphs have no screen-reader announcement independent of color. Confirmed by both assessments from different angles (LLM code read + a detector scan that couldn't even check contrast). Why it matters: this silently excludes any screen-reader-dependent staff member from a mandatory work tool. Fix: wire every label, add aria-label to icon-only status indicators. Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`

- **[P1] The review screen has no answer to scale.** Live data on the running instance showed a single CV with 32 stacked Biography cards and 16 fully-expanded empty sections in one continuous scroll — nothing collapses or paginates. Why it matters: the reviewer has to wade through noise to find the handful of items that actually need judgment. Fix: collapse empty sections by default behind a "16 sections not found (show)" toggle; cap or virtualize long item groups. Suggested command: `/impeccable distill`

- **[P1] Generate and Download are spatially disconnected.** The primary "Generate MDX CV" button sits at the top of the page; the resulting download link renders inside the Quality Report card, below a 21-row section checklist. Why it matters: the single highest-stakes moment in the product (the document that goes to HR) has a payoff that's easy to miss on first use. Fix: surface the download control right next to/in place of the Generate button once generation succeeds. Suggested command: `/impeccable polish`

- **[P2] Typography has drifted beyond DESIGN.md's declared scale.** The detector found 16 real CSS font-sizes (0.72rem through 1.2rem) that don't match the 3-step type scale DESIGN.md declares. Why it matters: this is honest signal that the design system just documented undersold the real granularity already in the CSS — either the scale needs more declared steps, or the sizes need consolidating; right now DESIGN.md and the implementation disagree. Fix: re-run `/impeccable document` to capture the real scale, or consolidate sizes onto the 3 declared steps. Suggested command: `/impeccable document`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (First-Timer):** Lands on 63 pre-scored items with no explanation anywhere of what HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW confidence or "auto-approved" means before deciding whether to trust it. The auto-approval threshold field on index.html accepts 0.01–2 with no visible relationship to the 0–100% shown everywhere else — typing "75" instead of "0.75" fails silently, no validation error.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent):** Every textarea, dropdown, and add-item field in review.html is unlabeled to a screen reader (confirmed by grep — zero instances). The only custom focus style in the whole system is the red outline on the currently-reviewed row; no equivalent visible-focus treatment exists for buttons or links beyond browser default.

**Riley (Stress Tester):** Live data shows real duplicate-upload noise already happening in production use — the same filename appears 10+ times in the upload history with no dedupe warning or search to isolate the right one. Clicking "Refresh" mid-typing in the command bar calls loadAll() with no unsaved-change check, silently discarding whatever was being typed.

## Minor Observations

- Reject and Delete use identical danger-red styling despite very different consequences (reversible vs. permanent).
- "Teach this heading" lists real person names (e.g. "AMAN MISHRA") in the same UI and with the same one-click permanence as genuine recurring headings like "DECLARATION" — a mis-click could corrupt classification for every future CV containing that string, with no review path for taught mappings.
- The one native confirm() dialog in the app (bulk-approve) is the only interaction that breaks out of the app's own visual system entirely.
- Photo upload restricts file type via accept but gives no size/dimension guidance in the UI.

## Questions to Consider

1. If 16 of 21 sections render fully-expanded "Not found" boilerplate on a typical CV, is the review screen surfacing the items that need real judgment — or making HR wade through noise to find them?
2. The data model already distinguishes reversible (reject) from destructive (delete) — why doesn't the interface?
3. Is the native confirm() dialog on bulk-approve — the one interaction outside the app's entire visual system — really the intended safety pattern, or a placeholder that never got a proper in-app modal?
